How to turn PDF pages into JPG or PNG

Last updated 10 October 2026 · Written by Souren Das

Some forms want a picture of a page, not the PDF: a profile photo upload that only takes JPG, a portal that asks for the signature page as an image, or a slide where you want one page of a report. PDF to JPG draws each page to an image with PDF.js in this tab and downloads one picture or a ZIP. The PDF is not sent to a FileTools Kit server.

When to convert, and when not to

Convert when the receiver only accepts images, when you want to post a page on social media or in a chat, when you need a page as a picture inside a slide or a document, or when you want to shrink a scanned PDF by rebuilding it from smaller images.

Do not convert when the receiver accepts PDF. A PDF keeps selectable text and usually prints sharper. A JPG of a page is a photograph of it: you cannot select or search the words, and this is not OCR, so it will not recover text from a blurry scan.

Steps

  1. Open PDF to JPG and press Choose a PDF.
  2. The page reads the file and shows the page count. The Pages box fills with every page, for example 1-12. Change it to the pages you need, such as 1 or 1-3,5.
  3. Pick a Format: JPG for photos and scans, PNG for flat forms with thin lines.
  4. Pick a Resolution: Screen, Standard, or Print. Standard is selected to start.
  5. Press Export JPG (or Export PNG). The page shows progress, such as Rendering page 2 of 3.
  6. Open the image or unzip the ZIP and check the result before you upload it.

Resolution and format

Screen renders at 108 DPI, Standard at 144 DPI, and Print at 216 DPI. An A4 page comes out about 893 by 1263 pixels at Screen, 1191 by 1684 at Standard, and 1786 by 2526 at Print. Standard is enough for most upload forms and for reading on a phone. Print is for a page someone will print or zoom into, and it makes files roughly twice the size of Standard. Screen is the one to choose when a form caps the image at 200 or 300 KB.

JPG is saved at quality 0.9, which keeps text readable in a picture of a page. PNG is lossless and larger, and it is the better pick for a form with thin lines or a page you plan to edit later. For a page that is mostly a photo, JPG is far smaller at the same look.

What you download

One page downloads as a single image. Two or more pages download as one ZIP named after the PDF, for example contract-jpg.zip. Inside, each image is named after the PDF and its page number with zero padding, such as contract-page-01.jpg, contract-page-02.jpg, so they sort correctly in a file manager. Unzip before you upload: most forms want the images, not the archive. The status line shows the ZIP size when it is done.

Long documents

Every page is drawn in this tab, one after another. Above 50 pages the tool warns that large documents take a while and use a lot of memory. Print size on a long document can slow an older laptop or a phone. Render in ranges of twenty or thirty pages, such as 1-25 and then 26-50, and close other heavy tabs.

A worked example

A portal wants the signed last page of a 6-page offer letter as a JPG under 300 KB. Choose the PDF, change Pages from 1-6 to 6, keep Format on JPG, and choose Screen. Press Export JPG, and offer-letter-page-6.jpg downloads, typically a few hundred KB or less for a text page at that size. If it is still over the limit, open it in Image Optimizer, set Output Format to JPEG, and lower Visual Quality until the row shows a size under the cap. Check that the signature is still clear before you upload.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • No valid pages in that range. The numbers are past the end of the file or mistyped. Use page positions from 1 to the count shown.
  • Could not render these pages. Try Screen or Standard, or a smaller range. A password-protected PDF often will not open; unlock it in a reader you trust first.
  • Image too big for the form. Pick Screen, or shrink the JPG in Image Optimizer with a lower Visual Quality.
  • Blurry small print. Use Standard or Print rather than Screen, or PNG for line drawings.
  • Uploaded the ZIP. Unzip it first and upload the single page the form asked for.

Privacy

FAQ

Can I turn the images back into a PDF?

Yes, with JPG to PDF. The new PDF will be image-only, without selectable text.

Why is the PNG so much larger than the JPG?

PNG keeps every pixel exactly. For scans and photos that costs a lot of space.

Can I choose a custom DPI?

No. The three presets cover screen, upload, and print use.

Does it keep links or form fields?

No. An image has none.
