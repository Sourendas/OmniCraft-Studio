How to turn PDF pages into JPG or PNG

Some forms want a photo of a page, not the PDF. PDF to JPG draws each page to an image with PDF.js in this tab and lets you download one picture or a ZIP. The PDF is not sent to a FileTools Kit server.

What you get

You get a picture. You cannot select the text in the JPG. This is not OCR and it will not recover words from a blurry scan. A higher scale makes a sharper image and a larger file. Export one page first and check it before you render a long document.

Password-protected PDFs often will not open. Unlock them in a reader you trust, then render the unlocked copy.

Steps

  1. Open PDF to JPG.
  2. Pick the PDF. The page range starts as every page, for example 1-12. Change it to the pages you need, such as 1-3,5.
  3. Choose JPG for a photo-like page or PNG for a flat form with sharp lines.
  4. Download and open the image. If a portal caps size, shrink the JPG in Image Optimizer.

Resolution and format

There are three sizes. Screen renders at 108 DPI, Standard at 144 DPI, and Print at 216 DPI. An A4 page comes out about 893 by 1263 pixels at Screen, 1191 by 1684 at Standard, and 1786 by 2526 at Print. Standard is enough for most upload forms. Print is for a page someone will print or zoom into, and it makes files roughly twice the size of Standard.

JPG is saved at quality 0.9, which keeps text readable in a photo of a page. PNG is lossless and larger, and it is the better pick for a form with thin lines or a page you plan to edit later.

What you download

One page downloads as a single image. Two or more pages download as one ZIP named after the PDF, for example contract-jpg.zip, with one image per page inside. Unzip it before you upload, because most forms want the images, not the archive. A long document at Print size uses a lot of tab memory. If the tab slows, render in ranges of twenty or thirty pages.
