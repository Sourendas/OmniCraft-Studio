Resume format for freshers: an ATS-friendly one-page PDF

Last updated 10 October 2026 · Written by Souren Das

Your first resume is hard to write because the usual advice assumes you already have a career. Freshers, final-year students, and people switching fields have projects, internships, coursework, and part-time work instead. This guide explains what a fresher resume should contain, how applicant tracking systems (ATS) read a PDF, and how to build a clean one-page resume in Resume Builder without padding or tricks.

What an ATS actually does

Many companies, especially larger ones and recruitment agencies, receive applications through software that stores resumes and lets recruiters search them. The software extracts text from your file into fields such as name, email, job titles, and skills. A recruiter then searches or filters, for example for "Python" and "SQL", and reads the resumes that come up.

So the practical goals are simple. The text in your PDF must be real, selectable text in a sensible reading order. Your headings should be ones the software recognises, like Experience, Education, and Skills. And the words a recruiter would search for should appear naturally where they are true. Nobody outside the vendor knows exactly how each system scores resumes, so be wary of anyone promising a "pass rate".

What a fresher resume should contain

Keep it to one page. In order:

  1. Contact. Name, email you check daily, phone with country code, city. Add LinkedIn and GitHub or a portfolio if they show real work. No photo, date of birth, religion, or marital status unless a specific employer asks.
  2. Summary. Two or three lines on what you are looking for and what you bring. "Final-year B.Tech (CSE) student looking for a backend developer role. Built and deployed two Flask projects with PostgreSQL." is better than "Hard-working and passionate individual."
  3. Education. For a fresher, education often comes before experience. Degree, field, college, year, and your CGPA or percentage if it helps you.
  4. Experience and projects. Internships, college projects, freelance jobs, hackathons, volunteer work, and part-time jobs all count. Use two to four bullets each.
  5. Skills and certifications. Tools and languages you can actually use in an interview, and certifications you completed.

Writing bullets that work

A good bullet says what you did, how, and what came of it. Start with a verb, name the tools, and give a number only if it is real.

  • Weak: "Worked on a website for a college fest."
  • Better: "Built the registration site for our college fest in React and Firebase; handled sign-ups for all events over two weeks."

If you do not have a number, do not invent one. Describe the scope instead: how many people used it, how long it ran, what you were responsible for. Interviewers often ask about one bullet in detail, so write only what you can explain.

Building it in Resume Builder

  1. Open Resume Builder. It opens with a sample person filled in so you can see every section. You will replace all of it.
  2. Pick a layout in the template picker. For ATS-heavy applications, a single-column layout such as Classic, Ivy, or Compact keeps the reading order simplest. Sidebar and Timeline look good when a person reads the PDF, and they still produce real text, but a single column is the safest choice if you are unsure.
  3. Fill in Contact: full name, job title (the role you are applying for, such as "Junior Data Analyst"), email, phone, location, and LinkedIn, GitHub, or website if relevant. Leave a field empty to keep it out of the PDF.
  4. Write the Summary.
  5. Under Experience, press Add role for each internship or project. New roles are added at the top, so add the oldest first and the newest last, and the most recent ends up first. For a project, put "Project" in the job title, for example "Project: Library management app", and your college as the company. Tick Current role if it is ongoing. Press Add bullet for each line.
  6. The Rewrite button next to a bullet adds an action verb at the start if it does not already have one. Read the result: it cannot add facts and may not choose the best verb.
  7. Under Education, add your degree with Add school. The GPA field is optional.
  8. In Skills & certifications, type skills and certifications as comma-separated lists.
  9. Optionally paste a job description into Keyword overlap. It compares a fixed list of common technical terms in the posting with your resume and shows which appear in both. It is a local check in your browser, not an employer's ATS. Use it to spot a skill you have but forgot to list, not to stuff in words you cannot back up.
  10. Press Download PDF. The file is named after you and the template, such as Priya_Sharma_classic.pdf.

Check the PDF before sending

  • Open it and press Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on a Mac). Search for your own name and a skill. If the search finds them, the text is real and machine-readable.
  • Search for words from the sample person to make sure nothing is left over.
  • Check it fits one page. If it spills over by a few lines, try the Compact template or tighten the bullets.
  • Check the email and phone number twice. A typo there costs you every callback.

Common mistakes

  • Two-page fresher resumes. Recruiters skim. Cut older school-level achievements unless the job asks for them.
  • Listing every technology you have heard of. If you cannot answer a basic question on it, leave it out.
  • Keyword stuffing. Copying the job description into your resume, or hiding white text, may get you found and then rejected by the person reading it.
  • Images of text. A resume exported as an image, or designed in a tool that flattens text, cannot be read by an ATS.
  • Unprofessional email addresses. Create a simple name-based address if yours is from school days.
  • Sending the same resume everywhere. Change the job title and the summary for each type of role. It takes five minutes.

FAQ

Is a PDF or Word file better for an ATS?

Most systems handle both. Use what the posting asks for. If it does not say, a PDF with real text keeps your layout intact.

Should I include a photo?

Not unless the employer asks. Resume Builder does not add photos.

What if I have no internships?

List college projects, online course projects with code you can show, hackathons, and any part-time or family business work. Be specific about what you did.

Can I write my resume in Hindi or another Indian language?

The PDF uses built-in Latin fonts, so non-Latin scripts will not display correctly. Most private-sector roles in India expect English resumes.
